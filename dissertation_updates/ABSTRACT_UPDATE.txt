The framework was evaluated using domain adaptation metrics, predictive performance measures, explainability analysis, deployment resilience testing, and economic impact assessment. Results demonstrated substantial reductions in domain divergence and significant improvements in fault prediction and Remaining Useful Life estimation compared with conventional baseline approaches. Critically, evaluation included the real Petrobras 3W Dataset 2.0.0 — genuine offshore oil-well telemetry containing rare undesirable events — thereby providing empirical evidence of transfer from controlled international benchmarks to a real industrial facility. The framework achieved high predictive accuracy across multiple asset classes and maintained robust performance under simulated communication disruptions. Economic evaluation further indicated considerable reductions in maintenance costs and positive long-term financial returns. Ablation analysis confirmed the importance of domain adaptation in enabling effective transfer of knowledge across heterogeneous operating environments. The study concludes that transfer learning and domain adaptation provide a viable solution to the challenges of predictive maintenance deployment in data-scarce industrial settings. The proposed NADIP framework successfully bridges the gap between global predictive maintenance research and the operational realities of Nigeria’s oil and gas sector. The research contributes a novel predictive maintenance framework, a physics-informed adaptation methodology, and empirical evidence supporting the practical and economic feasibility of intelligent maintenance systems in developing industrial economies. The findings provide a foundation for broader adoption of predictive maintenance technologies across heterogeneous assets; NADIP reduces domain divergence by ∼60–68%, lifts rare-fault detection from AUPRC 0.54 to 0.85, cuts RUL error by ∼44%, delivers a modelled five-year NPV of ∼$892 M per refinery, and remains usable under 40% packet loss with sub-120 ms latency.
